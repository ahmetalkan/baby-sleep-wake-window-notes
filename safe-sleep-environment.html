Safe Sleep Environment Checklist for Infants

No wake-window adjustment matters as much as the physical safety of the sleep space. Both the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and the Eunice Kennedy Shriver National Institute of Child Health and Human Development (NICHD) publish clear baseline standards for every nap and nighttime stretch during the first year.

Core room and crib checklist

  • Back to sleep every time: Place the baby on their back for every sleep, both naps and night, until age one.
  • Firm, flat, bare surface: Use a safety-approved crib, bassinet, or play yard with a firm mattress and a fitted sheet only—no pillows, crib bumpers, loose blankets, or soft toys.
  • Room-sharing, not bed-sharing: Keep the baby's separate sleep space in the caregiver's bedroom, close to the bed, for at least the first six months.
  • Avoid inclined sleepers and routine car-seat naps: If an infant falls asleep in a car seat, stroller, or swing, move them to a firm flat surface as soon as practical.
  • Prevent overheating: Dress the baby lightly for the room temperature and skip hats indoors during sleep; wearable sleep sacks replace loose blankets.
