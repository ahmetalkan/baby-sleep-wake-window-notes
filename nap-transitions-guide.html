Nap Transitions Guide: Dropping Naps Without Overtired Battles

Across the first two years, babies steadily consolidate daytime sleep—moving from four or more brief newborn naps down to three, then two, and eventually one midday nap. Transitions rarely happen cleanly on a single day; alternating between two-nap and one-nap days for a week or two is ordinary.

Consistent signs a nap may be ready to drop

  • Repeated refusal of the final afternoon nap for 10 to 14 consecutive days despite a calm dark room.
  • Bedtime creeping noticeably later because the last nap pushes sleep pressure past evening.
  • New early-morning wakings or long cheerful awake periods in the middle of the night.

How to handle the messy middle week

Challenge during transitionPractical adjustment
Child cannot stay awake until usual evening bedtimePull bedtime 30–45 minutes earlier temporarily rather than adding a late catnap
One-nap morning stretch feels too longShift the first nap later by 15 minutes every few days instead of jumping two hours at once
Afternoon meltdown before dinnerUse quiet low-stimulation time (stroller walk, books, snack) to bridge to early bedtime
