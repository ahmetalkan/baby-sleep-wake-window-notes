Baby Sleep Wake-Window Notes: Tracking Sleep Pressure by Age

A wake window is the stretch of time an infant stays comfortably awake between waking from one sleep period and falling asleep for the next. Tracking awake spans alongside total 24-hour sleep helps caregivers read sleep pressure without treating any chart as a rigid clock.

The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) notes that sleep needs change rapidly during infancy as circadian rhythms mature and daytime wakefulness lengthens. Rather than chasing exact minute targets on day one, logging three to five ordinary days reveals your child's natural pattern.

What to log for three days before changing a schedule

Before shifting bedtime or dropping a nap, write down four observations for 72 hours. Structured age-stage sample schedules and calculators are published on Sleep Schedule Lab and its breakdown of wake windows by age.

Log columnWhat to notePattern it helps expose
Wake-up and sleep onsetActual time eyes open and actual time asleep (not just crib placement)True awake length vs time spent winding down
Pre-sleep moodCalm, brief fussing, or wide-awake babbling for 25+ minutesSeparates undertiredness from overtired stress
Nap durationShort single-cycle nap vs restorative longer napWhether earlier awake time was too short or too long
Night wakingsHunger feed vs brief stir vs long wide-awake split nightExcess daytime sleep or shifting total sleep need
